# Making Still easier to start — 1 October 2026

Still remains AI-free. These changes aim to reduce decisions and effort in the daily flow. They are product design choices, not a tested treatment for procrastination or a claim of 10/10 usability.

## What changed

- Today puts the action before the large image and progress details.
- Money and value visions open a dated entry directly, without editing the target first. Saving applies immediately; a failed save keeps the entry visible for retry.
- Milestone visions offer the next unfinished milestone with one-tap completion and Undo.
- Visions without a measurable plan offer their current small step, or help choosing one. Completion records proof without forcing a new planning form.
- Make it smaller offers editable starting examples and a when/if cue. The two-minute framing is an optional sizing prompt, not a scientifically established threshold. Completing a small step does not claim that a larger milestone or savings target is complete.
- Not today retains the step and expires after the local calendar day. No missed-day punishment or streak reset is introduced.

## Evidence and limits

Rozental et al. (2018), *Targeting Procrastination Using Psychological Treatments: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis*, found small overall benefits with substantial variation between studies. This does not establish that a specific app feature works: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30214421/

Gustavson and Miyake (2017), *Academic Procrastination and Goal Accomplishment*, found that brief SMART-goal and implementation-intention interventions did not significantly reduce academic procrastination over three weeks: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28943742/

These findings support a cautious approach: offer concrete actions and optional cues without promising a cure. No diagnosis, personality score, or AI inference is used.

## Validate with real users before claiming excellent usability

Observe users completing three tasks without help: record a deposit, complete and undo a milestone, and replace an intimidating step with a smaller one. Record task success, mistakes, time, and perceived effort. Then ask a small consenting group to use Still for two weeks and report whether it helped them start intended actions. Automated tests and mobile previews establish functionality and layout, not behavioral effectiveness.
